    How to read it

    The rules the regime line is built from. Direction matters more than level in every one of them.

    • M2Global M2 (US, euro area, China, Japan, converted to dollars) leads the S&P 500, gold and bitcoin by about ten to twelve weeks. A turn in the year-over-year rate is the earliest tell that money is about to look for a home.
    • NLFed net liquidity is the weekly pulse: balance sheet minus Treasury cash minus reverse repo. Rising means banks hold more reserves to lend and to warehouse assets; four falling weeks in a row has preceded most equity pullbacks since 2022.
    • RRPWith reverse repo near zero, a Treasury cash rebuild or a heavy auction month drains bank reserves directly. The same TGA rise that was harmless in 2023 now bites.
    • BCBank credit growing faster than nominal GDP means the private sector is creating money on its own, not just the central bank. This is what keeps an expansion going after QE stops.
    • CNChina credit impulse leads copper, miners and emerging markets by about six months. It is the single best early signal for the industrial metals and the miners row in section 3.
    • FXDollar down with M2 up is the strongest setup for commodities, gold and emerging markets. Dollar up with M2 down is the setup for cash and staples. The rotation detector uses this pair as its macro confirmation.
    • RESReserves are the binding constraint, not the balance sheet. Below about 9% of GDP repo rates jump above the rate the Fed pays on reserves; SOFR minus IORB is the daily tell and it moves days before anything else does.
    • BILLThe Treasury's issuance mix matters as much as the Fed. Bills above 20% of issuance are absorbed by money funds out of RRP and deposits, which is easing in disguise; coupon-heavy quarters drain reserves and lift term premium. The quarterly refunding announcement is a liquidity event, not a bond-market footnote.
    • FISA rising deficit injects money into private balance sheets (fiscal impulse) and supply into the bond market at the same time. The issuance mix decides whether it lands as growth or as term premium.

    What each number tells you

    • RYReal yields move inversely to gold, strongly and consistently since 2006. Falling real yields with a weak dollar is the gold and miners setup; rising real yields kill it regardless of inflation headlines.
    • 2s10A steepening curve driven by front-end cuts (bull steepener) helps banks, small caps and homebuilders. Driven by the long end rising (bear steepener) it hurts long-duration tech and real estate.
    • HYHigh yield spread above 400 bp confirms risk-off; above 500 bp is stress. Below 350 bp with tightening trend, the credit market is not worried, whatever equities do that week.
    • MOVEBond volatility above 120 spills into every market because dealers cut risk everywhere at once. Below 100 is the quiet regime where carry and momentum work.
    • DXYDollar down favors emerging markets, commodities and US multinationals. Dollar up with yields up is the setup that pulls money out of everything except cash.
    • MTGMortgage rate under 6.5% has historically revived housing turnover; homebuilders lead the rotation before the data shows it.
    • TPTerm premium separates why long yields move. Yields up with term premium up is supply and fiscal risk (bad for everything long duration); yields up with term premium flat is growth (fine for cyclicals). Today: yields down, premium up, the market is paying for Treasury supply.
    • 3m103m10y is the recession-model curve. Inversion is the warning; the re-steepening after it, driven by the front end collapsing, is when recessions historically started. Treat the first two quarters after re-steepening as the risk window.
    • 5y5y5y5y forward inflation is what the Fed watches. Anchored near 2.3% gives the Fed room to cut into a soft patch; above 2.6% it cannot, whatever payrolls do.
    • CCCCCC spreads decouple from HY before a credit event. HY tight with CCC widening is the classic late-cycle divergence.
    • SBCStock-bond correlation decides whether bonds hedge. Negative: 60/40 and risk parity work and lever up. Positive (2022 to 2024): everything sells together on inflation surprises, and cash and gold are the only hedges.
    • CORImplied correlation low with the VIX low means single stocks are moving a lot in opposite directions. That is a rotation market: sector and factor bets pay, index bets do not.

    How the detector works

    Five conditions, each a yes or no, scored daily per theme. Thresholds are starting points; they get tuned after three months of logged alerts.

    • 1Relative strength turn. The theme divided by the S&P 500 crosses above its 50-day average (entry) or below it (exit), or its 13-week relative strength rank jumps three or more places.
    • 2Flow acceleration. Four-week ETF net flow z-score above +1.5 against its one-year distribution; below -1.5 for an exit.
    • 3Breadth thrust. Share of members above their 50-day rises from under 35% to over 60% within four weeks.
    • 4Seasonal window. From today's date, the next eight weeks show a positive average return relative to the index and a hit rate above 60% over twenty years.
    • 5Macro confirmation. The theme's own driver agrees. Gold and miners: real yields and the dollar falling. Banks, small caps, homebuilders: curve steepening, mortgage rates falling. Cyclicals and copper: copper to gold ratio rising, China credit impulse up. Energy: crude above its 200-day, OPEC+ tightening. Staples and utilities: ISM under 48 or HY spreads widening. Semis and mega-cap tech: liquidity expanding, real yields falling. Emerging markets: dollar falling, China credit impulse rising.
    Alert rules: an alert fires on a state change, never on a level. States need three consecutive daily closes to flip; one alert per theme per week. Relative strength is measured on vol-adjusted returns (divided by 60-day realized volatility) so high-beta themes do not win by construction. The seasonal condition carries half weight. Every alert carries its first date, the signal list, and the backtest for the same signal count: hit rate over the next eight weeks, average excess return, worst case, t-statistic, and the same figures conditioned on the current cycle quadrant. Evidence bar: nothing is Confirmed below n of 20 and t of 2, whatever the signal count. Alerts are logged for tuning after three months. Live in v2: relative strength, the seasonal window and macro confirmation are scored for every theme; breadth where a free holdings file exists (the SPDR funds); flows are not scored until a free share-count feed exists; the relative strength rank-jump clause is not implemented. Backtests use the three conditions with twenty-year history, so a live count that includes breadth is compared with "all three".

    How the score works

    • SMSmart money score: average of three-year z-scores for hedger positioning (sign inverted so long reads positive), fund manager cash (inverted), insider ratio, dark pool buying.
    • CRCrowd score: average z-scores for the survey spread, Fear and Greed, small-trader call share, margin debt growth, search interest, speculator positioning.
    • ΔDivergence = smart money minus crowd. Alerts at +1.5 and -1.5. Extremes resolve over 8 to 12 weeks, so an alert is a conviction sizer, not a timing tool.
    • !Rule of use: sentiment never triggers anything on its own. It sizes a rotation alert. A Developing rotation with divergence above +1.5 is the best setup on the page; the same rotation with divergence below -1.5 gets skipped.
    • COTFutures positioning, done properly: the crowd is managed money on a three-year COT index (0 to 100). Commercials in gold, silver and crude are producer hedges, so their level says little; only the four-week change in commercial shorts counts as smart money there.     The quant standard applied here

    What separates a dashboard from a screen full of prices. Each rule is cheap to implement and expensive to skip.

    • 1Standardize everything. Every reading is shown with its three-year z-score or percentile (five years for monthly series), winsorized at plus or minus 3. Levels stay visible next to the transform so nothing hides behind it.
    • 2Composites are equal-weighted. Optimized weights overfit; four equal-weighted z-scores per composite, membership fixed in advance, changed only with a written reason.
    • 3Condition on regime. Two direction-based axes (growth and inflation momentum) plus liquidity place the cycle; every hit rate is reported unconditionally and for the current quadrant, and the alert shows the lower of the two.
    • 4Evaluate signals properly. Hit rate, average excess return, t-statistic, information coefficient (rank correlation of signal and the next eight-week return), decay profile from one to thirteen weeks, and turnover. Nothing is Confirmed below n of 20 and t of 2.
    • 5Event studies, not anecdotes. Surprise z = (actual minus consensus) divided by the dispersion of forecasts; average one-day and five-day moves per asset, split by sign, with n; the options-implied move is the bar for "big".
    • 6Seasonality is de-trended and weighted half. Subtract the unconditional mean, show hit rate and t-stat, never use it alone. Calendar effects with a mechanical cause (tax dates, expiry, rebalancing) rank above pure seasonality.
    • 7No look-ahead. Backtests use publication dates, not reference dates (July M2 is known in late August); walk-forward evaluation; 10 bp transaction cost assumed; the live alert log is the out-of-sample test.
    • 8Vol-adjust before comparing. Relative strength and momentum are measured on returns divided by 60-day realized volatility, so high-beta themes do not win by construction.
    • 9Show the timestamp. Every panel carries its data date; a panel older than its refresh interval greys out rather than showing a stale number as current.
