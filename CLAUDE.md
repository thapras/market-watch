## What this is
A personal global-markets monitor: is money supply growing, where is it sitting, where is it starting to rotate, what events could move it, and how smart money is positioned versus the crowd. Single static page (`index.html`), six questions in reading order, plus a build plan.

## Stack / constraints
- `index.html`: one self-contained page, no build step, no libraries. Google Fonts (Archivo, JetBrains Mono) with system fallbacks. Theme-aware (light and dark tokens). It reads `data/latest.json` (or an inline `#mw-data` block in the artifact copy) and fills every element carrying `data-tile`, `data-strip`, `data-cell` or `data-rank`; unfilled elements are tagged sample, failed feeds carry forward stale, readings older than their refresh interval (loader LIMIT: daily 10 days, weekly 16, monthly 110) grey out with their date.
- `fetch/` (v4, current): Python 3.9+, standard library only, no API keys, no pandas or yfinance. Sources: FRED fredgraph CSV, Yahoo chart endpoint, ECB data portal, BIS policy rates, EIA history tables, Cboe history CSVs, Treasury FiscalData, BoJ time-series tables, multpl, the ICI weekly release, the Cleveland Fed nowcast file, CFTC positioning through the Socrata API, Cboe daily put/call JSON, the AAII page, FINRA margin statistics, SqueezeMetrics DIX and GEX, alternative.me; for the calendar, Forex Factory's public JSON (this week only, no actuals), the Fed's FOMC calendar and historical pages, the ECB and BoJ meeting pages, the BLS release archives and schedules, TreasuryDirect's upcoming auctions and the Cboe delayed SPX chain (about 12 MB). Run `MW_CACHE=.cache python3 -m fetch.fetch` locally (12-hour response cache; the first run also pulls about 600 member prices for breadth, so allow five minutes), `python3 -m unittest fetch.test_compute fetch.test_v2 fetch.test_v4` for the rules, the page parsers, the detector, the regime and the calendar logic. Nightly on GitHub Actions at 05:30 Bangkok. Free data only; no backend, no database, no paid feeds.
- FRED sits behind Akamai: a browser-imitating User-Agent stalls or is reset; the plain client UA in `sources.py` passes. Do not "fix" a FRED timeout by adding a Mozilla UA.
- v2 state rules live in `fetch/v2.py` and `fetch/changes.py`: `data/state.json` is the run-to-run memory (detector states with the raw-state count behind the three-close rule, flags, quadrants, reads, score history), `data/changes.json` the ninety-day alert log. A same-day rerun does not count as a second close; the first run logs nothing. Never hand-edit those files; delete `data/state.json` to reset the log deliberately.
- Section 6 rules (v3): divergence = smart money minus crowd, each an average of three-year z-scores from the inputs with a free feed; producers' level says nothing in gold, silver and crude, so their four-week short change is the smart money input there; the semis row uses Nasdaq 100 futures as its proxy; alerts need two consecutive weekly readings beyond 1.5 and the line stays at 1.5 until the log says otherwise. Put/call, AAII and margin debt histories accumulate in `data/state.json` (the first run seeds a year of put/call sessions, about 250 requests once).
- Section 5 rules (v4): the tier table on the page is the contract (`fetch/calendar.py` ROWS): a new event type changes both together, everything else stays off. Dates in order of trust: a feed that confirms them (Forex Factory for the week, the central bank pages, the BLS lists, TreasuryDirect), then the usual-slot rules (ISM first and third business day, payrolls three Fridays after the reference week with the New Year slip, the QRA on the Wednesday nearest the first of the month), then the mechanical dates from the calendar itself; a rule row is marked "usual slot, to confirm" until a feed confirms it, and inside the week the feed wins. Time zones are fixed rules (US and EU daylight saving), no tzdata. Implied moves: at-the-money vol per expiry from the SPX chain, variance between consecutive daily expiries is one session's; only sessions bracketed by daily expiries get a figure, the median of the next ten is the bar. Surprises: consensus from Forex Factory, the first print from FRED on the run after release (reference period from the lag in SURPRISE, 21 days to resolve), standardized by the logged dispersion once twelve exist and by the documented prior scale before that; the index is the ninety-day mean and scores at six. Event studies are unconditional until the log holds 24 releases. `data/briefs.json` is written by hand through `/brief` and only read by the fetcher; never generate it from rules and never overwrite a reviewed entry. Earnings dates have no keyless feed and are not listed.
- Source quirks worth knowing before touching `sources.py`: EIA's CSV and XLS downloads redirect to signed CloudFront URLs, so the fetcher reads the plain HTML history tables instead; the BIS China policy rate series is the one-year loan prime rate, and BIS files carry NaN on weekends; the Cleveland Fed nowcast file is about 7 MB (one chart per month since 2013); China's M2 has no free monthly feed (NBS blocks non-browser clients, the DBnomics mirror lags months), so the M2 aggregate is US plus euro area plus Japan and says so; CNN's fear and greed endpoint answers bots with a 418, NAAIM renders its number in JavaScript, Cboe's CSV listings are closed but its per-day JSON is open; the CFTC annual zips are 19 MB each, which is why positioning uses the Socrata API instead.
- The page must be served over http to load the data file; `file://` shows placeholders only. Playwright: if element screenshots time out on "waiting for element to be stable", close and reopen the tab.
- Scope guard: regime and rotation monitor only. No trade execution, no single-stock analysis, no price targets.
- Every number shown as a placeholder must be labeled as such until the feed replaces it.

## Project-specific preferences
- Keep the six-question structure; a new panel must answer one of the six or it does not go in.
- Detector thresholds (RS 50-day cross, flow z 1.5, breadth 35 to 60, seasonal hit rate 60%, macro driver table) stay fixed until three months of alert logs exist, then tune from the log. The ranking's price-rule thresholds (200-day trend, 12-1 momentum, 50/200 state, 52-week high, breadth) and its 0.5 read band follow the same rule.
- Technical inputs are rule-based and computed from price data the fetcher pulls. No discretionary chart patterns and no pattern reads from external sites: anything that cannot be backtested and refreshed nightly stays off the page. Conditions and price are scored separately (trend is not a conditions pillar) so their agreement means something.
- Alerts fire on state changes only, never on levels; states need three consecutive closes.
- Ranking price rules follow the page's foot text exactly. Two deliberate deviations, both stated on the page: rule B (breadth stand-in, ratio to the S&P 500) is not applied to the two bond rows; the failed-breakout tag keys off the 55-day level, not the 20-day, because in a trend every up day is a 20-day high.
- Notifications are a diff of state changes against the reader's last read per section (localStorage on the static page), in three tiers: must read, notable, FYI. Must read is a few per month or the thresholds are wrong. The digest goes out only when there is a must-read; push (Telegram bot or ntfy, free) carries must-reads only. Never notify on a level or predict a move; "something coming" means scheduled tier-1 catalysts and near-a-flip setups.
- `[GLOBAL?]` Never truncate copy with an ellipsis. Labels wrap (with reserved height so figures stay aligned) or get shortened; a reader must never see "..." where text should be.
- `[GLOBAL?]` No em dashes, en dashes, connector hyphens or middots as separators in any copy, including UI labels and data notes. Use commas, colons, parentheses or "to" for ranges.
